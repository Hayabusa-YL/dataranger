\begin{abstract}
Data teams are starting to let software fix the data problems their monitors find, and the risk is a fix that satisfies every monitor while leaving the data wrong. We present DataRanger, a repair agent built around a gate that applies a fix only when it matches evidence the fix cannot influence: the control totals a source system publishes and the table's declared invariants. The gate's threshold is chosen with Learn-then-Test on a randomly sampled, fully adjudicated cohort of past incidents, which bounds silent harm, the share of incidents that end with a wrong fix applied, below a target the team sets, with high probability. A repair memory reduces harm when the incident mix changes, without a formal guarantee. We also release DQ-Repair, a benchmark of pipeline incidents with decoys, stale control totals and ground-truth fixes. Over 6,600 incidents, the gate kept harm within its target in at least 95\% of calibration splits while handling 59 to 79\% of incidents without a person, against 24 to 31\% harm for repair checked only by monitors or rules. On 4,299 incidents injected into TPC-DS it automated 78 to 91\% with mean harm below target. A hand-set tolerance matched the gate on one source and broke a 1\% target in every split on another. Open-weights language models proposing fixes caused 36 to 42\% harm when applied directly and over 5\% with a self-check, and stayed under target through the gate. On Hospital, Flights and Beers, the gate made fewer wrong changes than Baran or conformal data cleaning.
\end{abstract}
